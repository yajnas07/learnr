Cheatsheet

Metals & Non-Metals · Science Ch.3 · Quick Reference

Physical Properties — Metals

  • Lustrous — shiny surface when freshly cut
  • Malleable — can be beaten into thin sheets (Au, Ag most malleable)
  • Ductile — can be drawn into wires (Au most ductile — 2 km wire from 1 g)
  • Good conductors of heat & electricity (Ag > Cu > Au > Al)
  • Sonorous — produce ringing sound when struck
  • Generally hard, high melting/boiling points
  • Exceptions: Na, K — soft (cut with knife); Hg — liquid; Pb, Na — poor conductors

Physical Properties — Non-Metals

  • Non-lustrous (exception: iodine, diamond)
  • Brittle — break when hammered
  • Non-ductile, cannot be drawn into wires
  • Poor conductors (exception: graphite conducts electricity)
  • Generally soft, low melting/boiling points
  • Exist as solids, liquids, or gases at room temp (Br₂ = liquid)
  • Low density compared to metals

Chemical Properties of Metals

1. Metal + O₂ → Metal Oxide (basic) | 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O | 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
2. Metal + H₂O → Metal hydroxide/oxide + H₂ | 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑
3. Metal + Dilute Acid → Salt + H₂↑ | Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑
4. Metal A + Salt of B → Salt of A + Metal B (if A > B in reactivity)
HNO₃ doesn't give H₂ — it's an oxidising acid (oxidises H₂ → H₂O). Aqua regia (3HCl + HNO₃) dissolves Au & Pt.

Ionic Bonding

  • Metals lose electrons → form cations (Na → Na⁺ + e⁻)
  • Non-metals gain electrons → form anions (Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻)
  • Electrostatic attraction between ions = ionic bond
  • Example: Na⁺Cl⁻ — transfer of 1e⁻ from Na to Cl
Na (2,8,1) → Na⁺ (2,8) + e⁻  |  Cl (2,8,7) + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (2,8,8)

Properties of Ionic Compounds

  • High melting & boiling points — strong electrostatic forces
  • Hard but brittle — rigid lattice, layers crack on shifting
  • Solid state: don't conduct electricity (ions fixed)
  • Molten/Aqueous: conduct electricity (ions free to move)
  • Soluble in water, insoluble in organic solvents (kerosene, petrol)

Extraction of Metals — Steps

  • Step 1: Concentration — Remove gangue (hydraulic washing, magnetic separation, froth flotation)
  • Step 2: Conversion to oxide — Roasting (sulphide ores in air) or Calcination (carbonate ores without air)
  • Step 3: Reduction — Using carbon/CO for mid-reactive metals; Electrolysis for highly reactive metals
  • Step 4: Refining — Electrolytic refining (anode = impure, cathode = pure, electrolyte = metal salt solution)

Corrosion

  • Corrosion = surface of metal attacked by air, moisture, acids, etc.
  • Rusting of iron: Fe + O₂ + H₂O → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (hydrated iron oxide)
  • Needs BOTH O₂ and moisture; salt/acid accelerates it
  • Silver tarnishing: reacts with H₂S in air → Ag₂S (black)
  • Copper turning green: reacts with CO₂ + H₂O → CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂

Prevention of Corrosion

  • Painting / Oiling / Greasing — physical barrier
  • Galvanisation — zinc coating (sacrificial protection)
  • Electroplating — layer of Cr, Ni, Sn on surface
  • Alloying — stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni + C)
  • Anodising — thickening Al₂O₃ layer on aluminium
