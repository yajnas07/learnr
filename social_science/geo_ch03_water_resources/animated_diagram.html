Water Resources

Water scarcity, multi-purpose dams, rainwater harvesting and sustainable water management

🌍 Water — A Renewable but Scarce Resource
Water is a renewable resource that keeps circulating through the hydrological cycle. Yet freshwater is scarce. About 96.5% of the world's water is in oceans (saline). Only about 2.5% is freshwater — most of it frozen in ice-caps and glaciers.

Distribution of Water on Earth

Oceans (saline)96.5%
Frozen ice-caps & glaciers1.75%
Groundwater0.68%

🔄

Hydrological Cycle

Water constantly renews through evaporation, condensation and precipitation. This makes it a renewable resource.

🇮🇳

India's Share

India receives ~4% of the world's annual precipitation, holds ~2.5% of world's water — yet supports ~18% of world population.

🌧️

Uneven Rainfall

India's rainfall is highly seasonal & uneven — most falls in a few monsoon months, causing floods and droughts.

📉

Falling Availability

Per capita water availability is declining fast due to rising population, agriculture and industrialisation.

🚱 Water Scarcity — Causes
Water scarcity is the shortage of water relative to demand. It may exist even in areas with plenty of water because of over-exploitation, unequal access and pollution. Scarcity is mostly human-made.
👥

Growing Population

More people means more water for drinking, farming and industry. Also raises demand for food — needing more irrigation water.

🌾

Over-Irrigation

Agriculture uses the most water. Water-intensive crops and free/subsidised power lead to over-pumping of groundwater.

🏭

Industrialisation

Industries need huge amounts of water and energy. Hydroelectric power and manufacturing put pressure on freshwater.

🏙️

Urbanisation

Cities and dense housing colonies with their own pumping devices over-exploit groundwater resources.

🕳️

Falling Water Table

Excessive tube-wells and pumping lower the water table, making groundwater harder and costlier to access.

🧪

Water Pollution

Domestic sewage, industrial effluents and chemicals pollute rivers and groundwater — good water becomes unusable.

⚠️ Key Idea: Water scarcity may be an outcome of large and growing population, but it is also caused by unequal access — a resource-rich region can still face scarcity due to bad management and over-exploitation.
🏞️ Multi-Purpose River Projects
A dam is a barrier across flowing water that obstructs, directs or slows the flow, creating a reservoir. Multi-purpose projects serve several needs at once — irrigation, electricity, water supply, flood control, navigation and recreation. Nehru called them the "temples of modern India."

Purposes Served by Dams

🌾

Irrigation

Stored water is released through canals to farms, boosting agricultural output round the year.

⚡

Hydroelectricity

Falling water spins turbines to generate clean electricity — a major renewable energy source.

🚰

Water Supply

Provide drinking and industrial water to towns and cities located far from the source.

🌊

Flood Control

Reservoirs hold excess monsoon water, reducing downstream flooding during heavy rains.

⛵

Navigation

Regulated flow supports inland water transport and movement of goods along rivers.

🐟

Fishery & Recreation

Reservoirs support fish breeding and become spots for tourism and water sports.

⚠️ Problems & Opposition to Dams
Multi-purpose projects have benefits, but they also raise serious ecological, social and economic concerns. Recent movements have questioned whether these projects truly help everyone equally.
🏞️

Regulate Natural Flow

Dams fragment rivers, making it harder for aquatic fauna to migrate and breed. Sediment gets trapped, causing poorer riverbeds downstream.

🪨

Sedimentation

Reservoirs collect silt and sediment, reducing storage capacity over time and creating rockier riverbeds downstream.

🌊

Induced Floods

Ironically, dams meant to control floods have triggered them by releasing excess water. Sediment build-up worsens flooding during heavy rain.

🏘️

Displacement

Large dams submerge land and forests, displacing local communities and tribal people who lose their homes and livelihoods.

⚖️

Social Inequality

Benefits (water, power) often reach large farmers and industries, while costs fall on the poor and displaced — causing social conflicts.

🧂

Land Degradation

Over-irrigation from dams causes waterlogging and soil salinity, reducing land fertility in canal-irrigated areas.

Movements Against Dams

Narmada Valley

Narmada Bachao Andolan

Led by Medha Patkar. Opposed the Sardar Sarovar Dam over displacement of tribal communities and environmental damage.

Gujarat

Sabarmati-basin Farmers

Agitations by farmers over inequitable distribution of water and benefits from irrigation projects.

General

Inter-State Disputes

Objections raised over sharing of river water and benefits — e.g., Krishna–Godavari and Cauvery water disputes between states.

🌧️ Rainwater Harvesting
Rainwater harvesting is the technique of collecting and storing rainwater for future use, or recharging groundwater. It is a low-cost, eco-friendly way to conserve water and reduce dependence on dams and canals.

🏜️

Tankas of Rajasthan

Underground tanks in almost all houses of Bikaner, Phalodi and Barmer. Connected to sloping rooftops that channel rainwater. Kept water cool in summer.

🌾

Gendathur, Karnataka

A village where households built rooftop rainwater harvesting structures to meet their water needs — a modern success story.

🎋

Bamboo Drip Irrigation

In Meghalaya, tribal farmers use bamboo pipes to drip-irrigate betel and black pepper plants efficiently.

🏠

Mandatory in Cities

Many states (e.g., Tamil Nadu) have made rooftop rainwater harvesting compulsory for all buildings by law.

🛡️ Conservation & Management of Water
To secure water for the future, we must conserve and manage it wisely — reducing waste, preventing pollution, recycling and reviving traditional systems. Water conservation is everyone's responsibility.
🌧️

Rainwater Harvesting

Capture rainwater from rooftops and open spaces to recharge groundwater and store for reuse.

♻️

Recycle & Reuse

Treat and reuse wastewater for gardening, industry and flushing — reduces demand on freshwater.

💧

Efficient Irrigation

Use drip and sprinkler irrigation instead of flooding fields — saves large amounts of water.

🚿

Prevent Wastage

Fix leaks, avoid over-use in homes, and adopt water-saving habits in daily life.

🧫

Prevent Pollution

Treat sewage and industrial effluents before releasing them into rivers and groundwater.

🏞️

Revive Tradition

Restore tanks, johads and step-wells; encourage watershed development and afforestation.

Key Takeaway: The chapter urges a shift from large centralised projects towards decentralised, community-based water harvesting. Conserving every drop today ensures water security for future generations.
