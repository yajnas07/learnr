Manufacturing Industries · Geography Ch.6 · Quick Reference

Importance of Manufacturing

  • Manufacturing = producing goods in large quantities from raw materials
  • Backbone of development; lifts & diversifies the economy
  • Modernises agriculture; creates jobs in secondary & tertiary sectors
  • Reduces regional disparity & poverty
  • Brings foreign exchange through exports
Share of manufacturing in GDP has been stagnant; goal is to raise it to 25%+.

Textile Industry

  • Largest industry in the organised sector
  • Cotton — Maharashtra & Gujarat; first mill Mumbai (1854)
  • Jute — Hugli basin, West Bengal; near Kolkata port
  • India is a major silk producer
  • Weaving decentralised: handloom, powerloom, mill
National Jute Policy 2005 — to boost jute productivity & farmer prices.

Iron & Steel Industry

  • Basic (heavy) industry — "backbone" of modern industry
  • Raw materials: iron ore : coking coal : limestone ≈ 4 : 2 : 1
  • Bulky & weight-losing → located near raw materials
  • Concentrated in the Chota Nagpur plateau
  • India = largest producer of sponge iron
Plants: TISCO (Jamshedpur), Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bokaro, IISCO (Burnpur).

Industrial Location

  • Located where costs least, profits highest
  • Factors: raw material, labour, power, capital, transport, market
  • Industries cluster (agglomerate) to share facilities
  • Regions: Mumbai-Pune, Hugli, Bengaluru-TN, Gujarat, Chota Nagpur
  • Industrialisation & urbanisation go together

Industrial Pollution

  • Air — smoke & gases (SO₂, CO), dust
  • Water — organic & inorganic effluents into rivers
  • Thermal — hot water harms aquatic life
  • Land & Noise — solid waste (fly ash, slag), machinery
Control: reuse/recycle water, treat effluents, fit scrubbers & precipitators. NTPC = eco-friendly model.

Important Exam Points — Quick Recall

  • First cotton mill — Mumbai, 1854
  • Jute industry — Hugli basin, West Bengal (near Kolkata port)
  • Iron & steel — basic industry; raw material ratio ≈ 4:2:1
  • TISCO — Jamshedpur (private); SAIL — public sector
  • Chota Nagpur plateau — hub of iron & steel plants
  • Bengaluru — Silicon Valley / electronic capital of India
  • Aluminium — from bauxite; 2nd most important metallurgical industry
  • National Jute Policy — 2005
  • NTPC — model environment-friendly power producer
  • 4 types of pollution — air, water, thermal, land/noise
