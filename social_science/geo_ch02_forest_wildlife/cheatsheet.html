Forest & Wildlife Resources · Geography Ch.2 · Quick Reference

India's Biodiversity — Key Stats

  • 47,000+ plant species (15,000 flowering — 6th in world)
  • 89,000+ animal species
  • 1,200+ bird species, 2,500+ fish species
  • 50,000+ insect species (many undiscovered)
  • India = one of 12 mega-biodiversity countries
  • Has 8% of world's species on 2.4% of land area
  • Hotspots: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, NE India, Andamans

Forest Cover in India

  • Total forest cover: ~21.67% of geographical area
  • Target: 33% plains, 67% hills (National Forest Policy 1988)
  • Very Dense: 3.02% | Mod Dense: 9.33% | Open: 9.26%
  • Top states: MP, Arunachal, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Maharashtra
  • NE states have highest % of area under forests
  • Mangrove forests: Sundarbans (largest in world)

Legal Classification of Forests

TypeShareFeatures
Reserved~54%Most restricted. No public activity. MP, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh

Reserved forests are most valuable for conservation. Unclassed forests are most vulnerable to encroachment.

Causes of Depletion

  • Agricultural expansion — largest cause (26,200 sq km cleared 1951-80)
  • Colonial exploitation — railways, ships, cover fell 40% → 19%
  • Mining — Doon Valley, Sariska examples
  • Large dams — 5,000+ sq km submerged (1951-80)
  • Shifting cultivation (Jhum) — cycle shortened, no regeneration
  • Overgrazing — 500 million livestock; fuelwood over-extraction
  • Forest fires, habitat fragmentation, pollution

Key Legislation — Timeline

  • 1972 — Wildlife (Protection) Act: banned hunting, created sanctuaries/parks
  • 1973 — Project Tiger launched (9 reserves → 53 now)
  • 1980 — Forest Conservation Act: restricted diversion of forest land
  • 1986 — Environment Protection Act: umbrella law, EIA mandatory
  • 1988 — National Forest Policy: 33% target, JFM introduced
  • 1992 — Project Elephant: protect elephant corridors
  • 2002 — Biological Diversity Act
  • 2006 — Forest Rights Act: tribal rights recognised

Important Exam Points — Quick Recall

  • Asiatic cheetah — last Indian big cat to go extinct (1952)
  • Amrita Devi — Bishnoi woman who died protecting khejri trees (1731)
  • Sunderlal Bahuguna — Chipko leader, "Ecology is permanent economy"
  • Jim Corbett — India's first National Park (1936, Uttarakhand)
  • 33% — target forest cover for plains (National Forest Policy 1988)
  • 21.67% — current forest cover of India
  • 12 — number of mega-biodiversity countries (India is one)
  • Forest Rights Act 2006 — recognised tribal rights over forests
  • IUCN Red Data Book — international list of threatened species
  • In-situ vs Ex-situ — in natural habitat vs zoos/gene banks
