🗺️ India — Land Use Pattern (% of Reporting Area)
Key Facts about India's Land Use:
• Total geographical area: 3.28 million sq km
• Data for only ~93% of total area (some remote areas not surveyed)
• Net sown area + fallow = total cultivated area ≈ 54%
• Forest cover target set by National Forest Policy: 33% — currently only ~23%
• Grazing lands are shrinking due to encroachment and conversion
• Increasing trend: land under non-agricultural uses (urbanisation, industry, roads)
• Concern: Net sown area is reaching its maximum — future food security depends on productivity, not area
⚠️ Land Degradation in India
India has approximately 130 million hectares of degraded land.
• Mining regions: Jharkhand, MP, Odisha — opencast mining leaves land scarred
• Overgrazing: Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP — livestock depletes vegetation cover
• Over-irrigation: Punjab, Haryana — waterlogging and salinisation
• Industrial effluents: Gujarat, Maharashtra — chemical contamination
• Deforestation: NE India, Western Ghats, Himalayan foothills

🛡️ Soil Erosion & Conservation
Soil Conservation Methods:
• Contour ploughing: ploughing along contour lines reduces water run-off on slopes
• Terrace cultivation: broad, flat steps on steep slopes — common in NE states
• Strip cropping: alternating strips of crops break the force of wind
• Shelter belts: rows of trees to protect crops from wind in semi-arid regions
• Plugging gullies: build small dams to slow water and trap soil in gullies

Potential Resource

Exists in a region but not yet utilised. Technology or investment is the missing link.

Reserve

Subset of actual resource. Technology available, but deliberately not used — saved for the future.

Example: River water for future hydroelectric power

Zimmermann's famous statement: "Resources are not — they become."
This means resources are defined by their function and utility, not by their physical existence. A rock is just a rock until someone discovers that it can be used as a tool, a building material, or a source of metal. Technology, culture, and economics determine what becomes a resource.
🌍 World Resource Distribution — Is it Fair?
The Global Resource Paradox:
• The world's richest 20% of population consumes about 80% of resources
• Many resource-rich nations (Africa, South Asia) remain economically underdeveloped
• India: states like Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, MP are mineral-rich but among the poorest
• Goa is resource-poor but developed; Jharkhand is resource-rich but underdeveloped
• This "resource curse" happens when extraction benefits corporations/governments but not local communities
♻️ Resource Planning in India — Three Stages
Resource Planning is a multi-level process in India:
• National level — integrated with Five Year Plans
• State level — state development boards identify and allocate resources
• District/local level — Panchayati Raj institutions manage local resources

The Challenge: India has unequal distribution of resources — some states are resource-rich but underdeveloped, while others are resource-scarce but developed. Planning must bridge this gap.
🌱 Sustainable Development — The Balance
"Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." — Brundtland Commission, 1987

• Rio Earth Summit 1992: Agenda 21 — global blueprint for sustainable development. 179 nations adopted it.
• Kyoto Protocol 1997: Binding commitments to reduce greenhouse gas emissions
• Paris Agreement 2015: Limit global warming to below 2°C (ideally 1.5°C)
• Gandhi's Warning: "Earth provides enough to satisfy every man's need, but not every man's greed."
